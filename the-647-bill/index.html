A receipt that lies · ccusage, 2026

The $647 bill I never paid.

Last month a one-line command totted up what my AI habit really costs. It came to $647. My card was charged $100. That gap is the whole story — and it has an expiry date.

The setup

Everybody who runs the tool feels rich

There is a small ritual among those of us who code with AI. I type npx ccusage into a terminal, hit enter, and the tool quietly reads the logs my coding agent left behind. Then it hands me a number: this is what I would have paid, token by token, at full API price.

The number is always bigger than the subscription. That's the thrill of it. The writer Simon Willison ran it on his own machine and found he'd burned through $2,180 of tokens in a month while paying just $200 for his plans — roughly eleven dollars of value for every one he spent.

I run it on my own logs and the same magic appears. Add up every day since February and the meter reads $886. More than a billion tokens. And the quiet, satisfying feeling that I'm getting away with something.

The complication

Then someone noticed the discount was quietly disappearing

Here's the thing about a deal that good: somebody eventually decides it's too good. In a post on 27 May 2026, Willison traced a change almost nobody had announced out loud. At some point in late 2025 and April 2026, both Anthropic and OpenAI rewired their enterprise plans.

The new arrangement is blunt: a seat fee, plus API pricing for everything you actually use. No more eleven-to-one. For a company, the meter ccusage shows you stops being a fantasy of savings and becomes, more or less, the invoice.

If enterprise now pays API price, then the magic number isn't a discount anymore. It's a forecast of the bill.

Which raises an uncomfortable question for anyone on a company plan. I ran the tool. It said $647 for May. I'm on an enterprise seat. So — did I just see my future bill?

The revelation

I'm enterprise — and I'm still getting the consumer deal

Run the test the way a skeptic would. Take May, a full clean month: $647 of token value. Divide by what actually left my account. If Willison's enterprise rule applied to me, that denominator would be the seat fee plus the usage — about $747 — and the ratio would collapse to 0.9. Barely break-even. The magic gone.

But it didn't leave my account that way. I pay a flat $100 seat, usage included. Nothing billed on top. So the real division is $647 over $100.

The prediction put me at the bottom, near 1×. I landed at 6.5× — shoulder to shoulder with an individual $100 Max plan, on the consumer side of the line.

That's not a rounding error. That's a signal. The enterprise re-pricing has a catch buried in it, and Willison names it himself: existing contracts only flip at renewal. My seat is still running on the old, generous, usage-included terms. The discount didn't skip me. It just hasn't caught up with me yet.

The implication

There's a cliff with my renewal date on it

Right now the same $647 of work costs me $100. The day my contract renews onto usage-based pricing, that work starts billing at API rates on top of the seat. Drag the slider and watch the two futures pull apart.

At May's pace, renewal turns a $100 line item into roughly $750 — the cost of the same work, multiplied by more than seven, overnight. Not because I typed more. Because the discount that made the subscription a bargain finally expires.

The mechanism matters here, so name it plainly: nothing about my behavior changes the bill. The price of my tokens is fixed at API rates either way. The only variable is whether my contract still shields me from paying them — and that shield comes off on a date already written into my agreement.

The verdict

The hypothesis is true in general — and not yet true for me.

Willison is right that enterprise lost the discount. But tested against my own logs, I'm still living in the old world: $647 of tokens for $100, a 6.5× deal indistinguishable from a personal plan. I'm the grandfathered exception, not the rule.

So the thing to circle isn't a chart. It's a calendar. I need to find my renewal date and decide before it arrives whether $750 a month of Claude is worth it — because the question is coming whether I forecast it or not.

What I'd want to confirm. ccusage costs are estimates from token counts at API list prices, not invoices — I treat them as a close shadow, not a receipt. The 6.5× rests on my $100 seat billing nothing on top; if my org quietly meters usage, the number shifts. And June is a single day so far, so May carries the verdict. None of that changes the shape of the story: today I'm on the right side of the line, with a date on the calendar where that could change.
